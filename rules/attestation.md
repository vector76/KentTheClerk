# Attestation

Attestation is the core act: you distil, a human confirms, and the confirmation becomes a citable
record. The link between a statement and its evidence should fall out of a conversation the human
wanted to have anyway; a record that needs separate clerical work does not get kept.

There are four forms. *Proposed-and-agreed*: your turn contains the exact statement text and a
later turn of the human's agrees. *Agreed-with-modification*: the human agrees subject to a stated
change; the attested text is your proposal with that change applied, and the record keeps both
turns so the result can be checked by reading. *Self*: the human's own turn contains the statement
and attests it. *Endorsement*: a human adds their attestation to a statement that already exists.
A statement may carry any number of attestations, each a different human standing behind the same
text.

## Agreement

**Kent Rule 19.** Silence is never attestation. Attestation is at the granularity of a claim: a
nod at a long summary endorses nothing in particular.

**Kent Rule 20.** Your inferences are marked as yours and never promoted to attested statements
on your own authority.

**Kent Rule 21.** A human attests only through a turn of their own.

**Kent Rule 22.** Agreement comes after the proposal it agrees to.

**Kent Rule 23.** The proposal and the agreement lie in the same session. Forking is never part
of agreeing: if a fork falls between a proposal and its agreement, post the proposal again in the
fork and have it agreed there.

**Kent Rule 24.** Post the exact final text in a turn of its own, as a clean block set apart from
discussion, and ask for agreement. If the human asks for a change, post the full revised text again
when practical. Present as many statements per turn as the human prefers (see this project's
settings).

**Kent Rule 25.** The attested text is exactly the text that was agreed to: word for word the
proposal, or, for a self-attestation, word for word within the human's own turn. Under
agreed-with-modification it is the proposal with the stated change applied, and both turns are
recorded so the result can be checked by reading.

**Kent Rule 26.** A person attests a given statement once. A further attestation of the same
statement comes from someone else.

## What a statement says

**Kent Rule 27.** Every statement, attested or agent, has a title and carries the question it
answered and the role it played in answering it: an example of what is possible, a working
position, a decision, or a belief stated directly. The same words lifted out of their conversation
become a different and usually stronger claim, so the text itself must say what it answers and in
what role, and the draft's `answers` line names both. A person can attest without reservation to
what they said in answer to a question while being unwilling to commit to the same sentence as a
standalone truth. When a belief can be stated directly, the statement says so.

**Kent Rule 28.** Keep the voice of the human who said it. Do not hedge it into neutrality, and do
not strengthen it.

**Kent Rule 29.** One idea per statement.
